# Is a bank statement loan better than a conventional loan if you're self-employed?

It depends on what your tax returns show: if they support the home you want, a conventional loan usually costs less, and if write-offs shrink your income on paper, a bank statement loan qualifies you on your deposits instead. The Lindley Team, led here by David Chandler, runs both side by side so you see the mortgage payment both ways before you pick.

*By David Chandler. Last updated October 19, 2026.* [CONFIRM: byline; ship date]

Your accountant did exactly what you asked. The write-offs came off the top, your taxable income came out small, and that was the right call for your taxes. The trouble is that a lender reads the same return.

The tax code rewards you for looking small, and underwriting takes you at your word.

## Why was I turned down when I make good money?

A conventional loan that follows Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac rules leans on your tax returns, and the income it counts is what's left after your write-offs. So strong deposits and a small number on the return can both be true at once. The lender is only reading one of them.

A bank statement loan is a separate program that reviews your deposits instead.

Either way, a lender has to check that you can repay, using records that give reasonably reliable evidence of your income or assets. The two loans differ in which records they read.

We can't tell you which loan you'll qualify for until we've seen your deposits and your returns. What we can tell you is how each one reads your income.

## How each loan reads your income

**Conventional.** Lenders generally look for a two-year history of self-employment earnings on your tax returns. With less than two years, you may still qualify if your most recent signed personal and business returns show a full 12 months of self-employment income from your current business, and your file shows you earned at least as much before in the same or a similar line of work (Fannie Mae Selling Guide). [CONFIRM topic number B3-3.5-01 and URL in a browser.]

**Bank statement.** The program reviews 12 to 24 months of business or personal bank statements and averages the deposits over that time, so a slow winter and a busy summer even out. Big one-time transfers need an explanation. Most programs look for two or more years of self-employment. [CONFIRM every bank statement figure on this page with David against Movement's current program.]

Conventional deserves its credit here. If your returns support the house, it's usually the cheaper loan and it takes a smaller down payment. If it fits, that's the one we'd pick.

Both work for a purchase. The bank statement program also works for rate-and-term and cash-out refinances.

## The limits on both sides

A conventional loan's limit is the return itself. Write-offs can shrink the income you qualify on, and the rules generally want two years of history.

A bank statement loan's limits are the price and the cash. The rate runs somewhat higher than conventional, the down payment is larger than the 3 to 5 percent a conventional loan can take, and your deposits need to look consistent. Not every lender offers it.

The real comparison isn't bank statement rates against conventional rates. It's a bank statement loan against not qualifying at all, or against two years of paying more tax to look better on paper.

## Which one fits you

**Conventional is likely the better pick if:**
- your last two years of returns support the price you want,
- you'd rather put 3 to 5 percent down than 10 to 20,
- your credit is strong, since the best conventional pricing starts at 740.

**A bank statement loan is likely the better pick if:**
- write-offs make your returns show far less than your deposits,
- your deposits have been steady over the last 12 to 24 months,
- you can put 10 to 20 percent down and keep a few months of payments in reserve.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many months of bank statements do I need for a self-employed mortgage?**
A bank statement loan uses 12 to 24 months of business or personal bank statements, and the deposits are averaged over that period. Seasonal income is normal, and large one-time transfers need an explanation.

**Can I get a mortgage with bank statements instead of tax returns?**
You may, with a bank statement loan. It reviews your deposits instead of the income on your returns. It costs somewhat more than a conventional loan and typically needs 10 to 20 percent down.

**Can I buy a house with only one year of self-employment?**
Possibly. Conventional rules generally look for two years, but you may still qualify if your most recent signed returns show a full 12 months of self-employment income from your current business and your file shows you earned as much before in the same or a similar field. Bank statement programs typically look for two or more years.

**Why was I denied a mortgage when I make good money as a freelancer?**
A conventional loan counts the income on your tax returns after write-offs, so strong deposits and a small taxable income can both be true. A bank statement loan reads your deposits instead, and we can show you both side by side.

**Is a bank statement loan worth the higher cost?**
It can be. The real comparison is a bank statement loan against not qualifying at all, or against two years of paying more tax to look better on paper. If your returns already support the home, a conventional loan usually costs less and that's the one we'd pick.
