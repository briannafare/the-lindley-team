# Publishing to The Lindley Journal

Everything you need in one page. You never touch code, and you can't break the site.

---

## Getting in

Go to **thelindleyteam.com/studio** and sign in with Google.

That's it. Bookmark it.

---

## Publishing a new article — 6 steps

1. Click **All articles** → the **＋** button (top right).
2. Fill in the **Content** tab:
   - **Headline** — what readers see. 50–65 characters is the sweet spot.
   - **URL** — auto-fills from the headline. Leave it alone unless it's ugly.
   - **Summary** — 1–2 sentences. Shows on the blog index and doubles as your Google description.
   - **Category** and **Author** — pick from the dropdowns.
   - **Hero image** — drag and drop. **Alt text is required** — describe what's in the picture.
   - **Article** — write the body. Toolbar has headings, bold, bullets, links.
3. Add 3–5 **FAQs** at the bottom of the Content tab. *(See "Why FAQs matter" below — this is the highest-leverage 10 minutes you'll spend.)*
4. Open the **SEO** tab. You can leave everything blank and it'll use your headline and summary. Fill it in when you want tighter control.
5. Hit **Publish** (bottom right).
6. The article is live at `thelindleyteam.com/blog/your-url` in about **2 seconds**.

## Seeing it before you publish

Click the **eye icon** in the top bar to open Presentation mode. You get the real, styled page side by side with the editor — and you can click any text on the page to jump straight to that field.

Drafts are only visible to you. Nothing is public until you hit Publish.

## The 21 articles waiting to be written

Click **Needs writing** in the sidebar. Those are the posts that currently have a headline, a summary, and nothing else — the live site shows "Full article coming soon" on all of them.

To fix one:

1. Open it. The headline, summary, category, date, and URL are already correct — **don't change the URL**, it's already indexed by Google.
2. Write the body in the **Article** field.
3. Add FAQs.
4. Go to **Settings** tab → switch **"Still needs to be written"** to off.
5. Publish.

It disappears from the Needs writing list. That's your progress bar.

## Why FAQs matter more than the article

When someone asks ChatGPT or Google "do I need 20% down in Portland," the answer gets assembled from pages with clearly-structured question-and-answer content. FAQs are how you get quoted.

**Write the question the way a person would actually say it out loud.**

- ✅ "Do I really need 20% down to buy a house in Portland?"
- ❌ "Down Payment Requirements Overview"

**Answer in the first sentence, then explain.** 40–90 words.

> **Q:** Can I get rid of PMI without refinancing?
>
> **A:** Yes. Once you reach 20% equity you can request PMI removal in writing from your servicer, and at 22% equity they're legally required to drop it automatically. Rising home values count toward that equity, so you may qualify sooner than your payment schedule suggests — a new appraisal is usually all it takes.

## Writing rules that actually move the needle

- **One idea per paragraph.** Two to four sentences. Long blocks lose people on phones.
- **Section headings every 250–350 words.** Use *Section heading (H2)* from the style dropdown. This is how AI tools and skimmers navigate.
- **Link to 2–3 of your own other articles.** Highlight text → link button → *Link to another article*. This is genuinely one of the highest-ROI things you can do and it costs 30 seconds.
- **Aim for 900–1,500 words** on a real topic. Below ~600 you're unlikely to rank for anything competitive.
- **Use the Callout box** for warnings and key numbers. Breaks up the wall of text.
- **Don't put a number in the headline you'll have to update** — "current rates" ages badly, "how rates affect your payment" doesn't.

## Compliance — read this once, then it's muscle memory

You're a licensed mortgage originator. The blog is advertising.

- Never state a specific interest rate or APR without the required disclosure. **Safest: don't quote rates in blog posts at all.**
- Don't promise approval, a specific payment, or a specific outcome.
- Don't say "lowest rates," "guaranteed," or "best" — those are claims you'd have to substantiate.
- Keep the NMLS footer intact (it's automatic, just don't ask anyone to remove it).
- When in doubt, run it past Movement's compliance team before publishing. Unpublishing is easy, but a screenshot is forever.

## Fixing a published article

Open it, edit, hit **Publish** again. Live in 2 seconds.

**Bump "Last reviewed"** on the Settings tab when you meaningfully update an old post. Google reads it as a freshness signal.

**Don't change the URL of a published post.** If you truly have to, tell Bri first so a redirect gets added — otherwise the old link 404s and you lose whatever ranking it had.

---

## Taking something down

Open the article → **⋮** menu (next to Publish) → **Unpublish**.

It disappears from the site immediately but stays in the Studio so you can bring it back. Don't use Delete unless you're certain.

---

## If something looks wrong

- **Published but not showing on the site** — wait 30 seconds and hard-refresh. If it's still missing after 2 minutes, message Bri.
- **Red validation warning** — an accessibility or SEO guardrail (usually missing alt text). It tells you exactly what's wrong. You can't publish until it's fixed.
- **Image won't upload** — it's over 20MB or it's a HEIC from an iPhone. Export as JPG.

## The weekly rhythm that actually works

Pick one day. Ninety minutes.

1. Open **Needs writing**, take the top one.
2. Write it. Don't polish — publish it at 80%.
3. Add FAQs.
4. Link it to two older posts.
5. Publish. Done.

One post a week clears the backlog in five months and gives you 21 real, ranking pages. Two a week and you're done by Christmas.
